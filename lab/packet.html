2 · What the manuals and photos already say

The VFD Tyler bought checks out on paper. Its manual names it G300-015-045A-2SB: 15 kW, 45 A out, single-phase 220–240 V in on terminals R and S. The spindle in delta wants 27.4 A continuous, 31.2 A at peak duty — covered with room. The 11 kW model is 30 A out, under that peak: one size up was the right call. The amps in VEVOR's listings (50 A, 68.5 A) are input current.
  • 400 Hz is locked by default. Maximum frequency F0.07 stops at 300 Hz until F8.32 is set to 1. Set F8.32 first, then F0.07 = 400. Nathan's settings sheet already has this.
  • The feed. Nathan's BoM fuses the drive at 100 A on #3 copper — 125% of its 68.5 A input rating. Whether Tyler's panel can give that circuit is sheet B's first line.
  • The spindle is confirmed by your plate photo and the HSD data sheet: ES 919A 2P 08,00 kW, card H1423H0224, 24,000 rpm marked as its maximum.
  • The axis motors are Vexta PK299-F4.5A steppers on Pacific Scientific 6410 drives — the same pairing as the 2007 AXYZ 4008 on the LinuxCNC forum.

Four notes on Nathan's BoM

  • Thermal switch → the control too. The BoM lands it on the drive's X4, which stops the spindle. HSD also wants the feed stopped, or the tool keeps pushing into the work. The drive's fault relay should reach a LinuxCNC input.
  • The fan is small and always on. 230 V AC, 39 W at 60 Hz — about a quarter amp. A 1 A fuse covers it; it is fed from the line, never from the drive's output, and runs whenever the machine is powered.
  • #8 motor cable against the spindle's connector. HSD's minimum for the phase pins is AWG 10. Check that #8 fits the connector's terminals before 50 ft of it is bought — sheet C asks for the old cable's size.
  • The 100 Hz floor is 6,000 rpm. Fine for aluminum and wood; steel wants slower. Worth a line to HSD before the floor is set.

The drives check out on paper. CS3E is a drive; each needs a stepper. Ruled likely on Nathan's recommendation: new NEMA 34 Leadshine closed-loop motors. NEMA 34 pairs with the CS3E-D1008 (30–100 V DC, 8 A), not the D507. LinuxCNC's Leadshine driver lists the D1008 and one forum user homed a NEMA 34 with it; only the dual-axis 2CS3E-D507 is marked tested, so one drive gets proven on the bench before four get a cabinet.
