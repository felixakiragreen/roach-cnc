2 · What the manuals already say

Do not order the VEVOR 220 V VFD yet. The spindle ran on 380 V. A 220 V drive gets full power out of it only if the spindle has the optional re-strappable terminal board — and on single-phase input the drive must also be sized for it. Three survey answers decide it: the spindle nameplate, the terminal board, the shop supply (sheet B and C).

The only 2-pole, 8 kW motor in the HSD manual is the ES919 card H1423H0224 — almost certainly what "2P 8.00 kW H1…" on the plate is. Its two connections:

  • The old Commander SE33400750 is a 380–480 V three-phase, 7.5 kW, 16.5 A drive — a match for the star column. So the machine lived on three-phase 380–480 V.
  • Star left as is on a 220 V drive → full torque only up to about 6,900 rpm, roughly 3.8 kW instead of 6.6, and weaker the faster it spins (my arithmetic from the card: 220/380 × 200 Hz = 116 Hz).
  • Delta on single-phase 240 V → the drive must deliver 31 A out and pull something like 60–70 A in (my estimate). Buyers of the 11 kW VEVOR report about half power on single-phase, and no manual for that exact unit turned up.
  • The spindle's other needs — fan on 230 V AC whenever powered, even idle · sensors are PNP, normally open, 10–30 V · thermal switch normally closed, must stop the spindle · never spin unless S1 says "tool locked" · never release the tool while turning.

Sources: refs/HSD Spindle Manual p.33 (card), p.80–81 (connectors), p.84 (terminal board), p.87–89 (inverter, fan), p.113–115 (sensors) · Commander SE ratings table and VEVOR buyer reports via web search, listed in the README's findings.

The drives check out on paper. CS3E is a drive, not a motor — each needs a 2-phase stepper, ideally Leadshine's matching CS-M motor with encoder. NEMA 34 motors pair with the CS3E-D1008 (30–100 V DC, 8 A), not the D507 (20–50 V DC, 7 A). LinuxCNC has a Leadshine driver that lists the D1008; one forum user homed a NEMA 34 with it. Only the dual-axis 2CS3E-D507 is marked tested, which is why one drive gets proven on the bench before four get a cabinet.

3 · The survey sheet — print from here

The rule: photo first, label second, unplug last. Wide shot, then close-up, then the nameplate square-on and readable. Number wires with tape flags on both sides of every connector before it comes apart. Write answers on this sheet; photos carry the rest.

A · The state of things

  • What has already been unplugged or removed?
  • Does the machine still power up? yes / no / unknown
  • Wide photos: whole machine, each side, the cabinet door open, the gantry, the spindle carriage
  • Any AXYZ serial plate on the frame — photo

B · Shop power (decides the VFD)

  • Supply at the machine: single-phase / three-phase
  • Voltage, measured or from the panel: V
  • Breaker feeding it: A · spare panel capacity?
  • Photo: the panel label, the breaker, the machine's old plug or disconnect
  • Compressor: pressure it holds psi · dryer or filter fitted?

C · Spindle (decides the VFD)

  • Nameplate, square-on, every character readable — model, code starting H1…, volts, hertz, amps, rpm
  • Open the small cover on the spindle's electrical box (two screws): is there a terminal board with movable links? Photo it as found
  • Tool holder plate: ISO 30 or HSK?
  • Both connectors, mated and unmated; count the pins in use
  • Manual release button on the spindle? yes / no
  • Air lines into the spindle: how many · tube size
  • Fan at the rear: present? spins freely?
  • Turn the shaft by hand: smooth / gritty / rumble
  • Tool holders and collets on hand: count

D · Old VFD (free answers, if it still lights up)

  • Nameplate photo
  • If it powers: step through every keypad parameter and write number → value on the back of this sheet. Max speed, motor volts, motor amps and rated frequency are the spindle's settings as AXYZ commissioned it
  • Photo every control wire on its terminals before removal
  • Braking resistor fitted? Photo its label

  • Push each axis by hand end to end: smooth / tight spots / grinding — X Y Z
  • Racks and pinions: worn or chipped teeth? Rails and bearings: rust, play?
  • Grab the spindle nose and rock it: any play?
  • Gantry: push one side — does the other follow, or rack?
